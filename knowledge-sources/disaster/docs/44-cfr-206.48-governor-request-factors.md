# 44 CFR § 206.48 — Factors evaluating a Governor's major disaster request

**Authority:** Stafford Act / FEMA regulation (Public Assistance and Individual Assistance).
**Use in DevConnect Labs:** Expert knowledge only — not legal advice, not an auto-score,
not a substitute for capturing the **governor declaration date** on Policy Desk (step 1).

When FEMA reviews a Governor's request for major disaster assistance, these are the
primary factors for recommending whether assistance is warranted. Other relevant
information may also be considered.

## (a) Public Assistance (PA)

1. **Estimated cost of assistance** — Federal + nonfederal PA cost vs statewide
   population (per capita impact). Historical indicator: ~$1 per capita (CPI-adjusted
   annually). Minimum threshold belief: ~$1M PA damages even for lowest-population states.
2. **Localized impacts** — County / local / Tribal concentrations can warrant help even
   when statewide per capita is unmet (critical facilities; very high local per capita).
3. **Insurance coverage in force** — Amount that is/should have been in force; reduces
   anticipated assistance.
4. **Hazard mitigation** — State/local measures that reduced damages (e.g. building codes)
   weigh in the evaluation; especially when mitigation pushed PA below the indicator.
5. **Recent multiple disasters** — Last **12 months**: Stafford declarations, Governor
   declarations, and State funds already spent.
6. **Other Federal programs** — Other agencies may meet needs more appropriately than FEMA PA.

## (b) Individual Assistance (IA) / Individuals and Households Program (IHP)

Federal IA is **supplemental** — it must not replace State/Tribal/local capacity.
Principal factors for IHP: **State fiscal capacity** and **uninsured home/personal
property losses**. If those are unclear, FEMA turns to the other factors below.

### (1) State fiscal capacity and resource availability

- **Fiscal capacity (principal):** ability to raise revenue — TTR (Treasury), GDP by State
  (BEA), per capita personal income by local area, other treasury limits.
- **Resource availability:** State/Tribal/local, NGO, and private-sector housing /
  donation / rental capacity (HUD FMR for affordable rentals within commuting distance).
- **Cumulative recent disasters:** last **24 months** (esp. current fiscal cycle) —
  Presidential PA/IA **and gubernatorial** declarations.

### (2) Uninsured home and personal property losses (principal)

PDA-style data: cause; jurisdictions and concentration; homes impacted and degree;
estimated cost; homeownership rate; % with sufficient peril-appropriate insurance;
other PDA data.

### (3) Disaster impacted population profile (Census / federal demographics)

Poverty; SSI/SNAP receipt; pre-disaster unemployment; age 65+; age ≤18; disability;
LEP (English less than “very well”); Tribal considerations not captured above.

### (4) Impact to community infrastructure (>72 hours)

- Life-saving / life-sustaining: police, fire/EMS, hospital/medical, sewage, water.
- Essential community: schools, social services, child care, eldercare.
- Transportation and utilities (uninhabitable/inaccessible housing, commodities, rebuild).

### (5) Casualties

Missing, injured, or deceased — heightened need signal.

### (6) Disaster-related unemployment

Survivors who lost work and do not qualify for standard UI (self-employed, service,
seasonal: tourism, fishing, agriculture); major employers affected.

## ACME / mortgage ops reading

- **Governor declaration first** — Policy Desk step 1; feeds “recent multiple disasters”
  and the overall Stafford narrative.
- **IA vs PA** — IA/IHP more borrower-facing; PA is infrastructure / public facilities.
- **Insurance** — Coverage in force cuts expected federal assistance and shapes loan
  triage (flood cert, hazard, SFHA).
- Do **not** invent PDA dollar figures, TTR, or casualties. Cite context data only;
  otherwise explain the factor qualitatively.
